The council reporting problem

Councils commonly assemble corporate and service-review reports from spreadsheets, line-of-business systems, email, document stores and officer updates. The problem is often the manual work required to reconcile claims, evidence, measures, ownership and approvals across those sources.

  • Repeated chasing: reporting teams request missing updates and attachments.
  • Weak traceability: executive claims do not always point to current evidence or an accountable owner.
  • Status inconsistency: a service can be reported as on track while its measure is below target.
  • Late surprises: material exceptions emerge during executive review.
  • Manual re-keying: staff copy information between spreadsheets, Power BI, documents and reporting tools.
  • Limited decision focus: reviewers see every update when they need the matters requiring judgement.

The product thesis

Council Reporting Studio acts as a controlled reporting overlay. Existing systems remain the sources of record. The overlay creates a common reporting record, tests the evidence behind each claim, routes exceptions to the right role and produces a defensible executive reporting position.

How the workflow operates

1Receive approved reporting export
2Normalise fields and owners
3Test evidence and measures
4Route exceptions for review
5Create executive outputs
6Preserve audit record

1 Intake

A user loads a CSV through file selection or drag and drop. The POC validates required columns before accepting it. Production can also accept approved spreadsheets, structured officer submissions, controlled file drops, a reporting inbox or an anonymised historical pack. APIs remain optional.

2 Normalisation

The system converts different source labels into a common record covering commitment, service or action, accountable owner, status, progress, evidence reference, evidence date, metric result and target.

3 Evidence and measure tests

The POC identifies missing evidence, stale evidence, unsupported completion, an on-track claim below target and statements such as improved that still need interpretation. Production rules would be approved by the council and Rachna.

4 Review and accountability

The interface explains each exception, its evidence state and owner. A reviewer records a decision rather than silently changing the source claim. The POC preserves that action locally; production would retain it in an authenticated audit history.

5 Executive output

The executive view updates from reviewed records and presents the reporting position, decision state and exception schedule. Review CSV, executive CSV and audit JSON can be downloaded, and the report can be printed to PDF.

The key transformation is from a messy operational update to a short, decision-ready exception list. The system tests every record and elevates the ones that require judgement.

Defensible advantages

Data principle

Use the minimum information required for the reporting decision. The first validation should use an anonymised historical pack. Production design must agree tenancy, hosting region, retention, deletion, access roles, records obligations, security review and permitted AI processing.

Notifications

The POC generates an in-product notification centre from current exceptions and routes messages to accountable areas. Production can add approved email, task, Teams or workflow notifications, configured by severity, role, due date and escalation.

White-label model

The service can be presented under RS Consulting, a joint identity or the council's brand. White labelling changes the client-facing identity; it does not transfer source code or C51 technical IP. Attribution, support, opportunity ownership, data handling and commercial terms require written agreement.

Why the partnership can work

Rachna brings direct council service-review and IP&R knowledge, understanding of reporting pain and relevant council relationships. C51 brings the configurable workflow, evidence and exception model, product operation and technical delivery. Together, one-off advisory work can become a recurring managed reporting service.

What would weaken it

  • Building broadly before one workflow and buyer are validated.
  • Unfunded custom development.
  • Promises about integrations, security or savings without evidence.
  • Giving away source code or exclusivity before repeatable demand.
  • Using Rachna only for leads instead of domain judgement and client management.

Current capability boundary

The POC uses synthetic information and browser storage. It does not yet provide hosted multi-user accounts, production persistence, real email delivery, identity integration, automatic document extraction, live system connections or certified council security controls.

Claims not to make yet

  • A council has adopted, purchased or validated the product.
  • The workflow has delivered measured savings.
  • It integrates with named council systems without testing.
  • AI independently approves evidence or governance decisions.
  • The POC satisfies council privacy, records, hosting or security requirements.
  • Proposed prices or partner shares have been accepted.
